Computer vision for industrial quality control

Computer vision models inspect products with precision beyond human capability. Through deep neural networks, we detect microscopic defects or irregularities in production lines, ensuring consistent quality standards and reducing product rejection.

This is especially useful in sectors such as pharmaceuticals or food processing, where automatic defect detection can prevent defective batches and improve product traceability.

Problems it solves

  • Manual visual inspection that is slow and varies by person and shift.
  • Defects reaching the customer because only a sample is checked.
  • High reject rates with no data on when and where the defect occurs.
  • Classic vision systems that fail when the product or lighting changes.

How we do it

  1. We define with the quality team what counts as a defect and which cases are borderline.
  2. We collect and label images of good and defective parts on the line itself.
  3. We train classification, detection or segmentation neural networks, depending on the case.
  4. We deploy the model at the line, with suitable camera and lighting, and connect it to the reject mechanism or the plant system.
  5. We store images and results for traceability and to retrain the model when the product changes.

Example applications

  • Food: product appearance, shape and colour, pack sealing and labelling.
  • Pharma: presence and integrity checks on blisters, vials and packs.
  • Metal and plastics: scratches, pores, burrs and surface defects.
  • Logistics: code and label reading, counting and parcel verification.

Frequently asked questions

How many images are needed to train a model?

It depends on how much the product and the defect vary. You can often start with a small set of well-labelled images and grow it with what is collected in production.

Does it keep up with line speed?

The system is sized for the line's throughput. During the assessment we validate camera, lighting and response time before deploying anything.

Can we reuse the cameras we already have?

Yes, if resolution and lighting are good enough. If not, we propose the smallest change needed.
